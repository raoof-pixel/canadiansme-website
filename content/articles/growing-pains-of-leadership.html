Building a team that can grow means creating an environment where people have the confidence, support, and opportunity to lead.

In the early stages of building a team, it’s natural to be involved in almost everything. You know your people, understand the work, and can quickly step in when something needs attention. For many leaders, being hands-on is simply part of getting the business off the ground.

But as an organization grows, that same approach doesn’t always serve the team as well. People wait for direction instead of moving forward. Decisions keep landing back on the same desks. Over time, progress depends less on the strength of the team and more on whether a few key people are available.

In my experience, one of the hardest parts of leadership is recognizing when the approach that got you here won’t get you where you want to go.

Growth requires a different kind of leadership

Smaller teams work really closely together. You watch how one another operates; you learn and pick things up through everyday tasks and activities. But over time, teams grow and bandwidth starts to stretch the quality time leaders can offer individual team members.

The best leaders not only share openly what’s next, but more importantly, the ‘why’ and the reasoning that brought them to that decision. That’s how you authentically transfer knowledge and share your style of leadership.

Be clear about priorities and show your team how their work connects to the bigger picture. Give them space to make decisions within their roles. They may not tackle things exactly as you would, but give them the space to figure it out. The best learning happens in the breakdowns, and the best leaders coach and guide you through them.

Recognize the people who make others better

Most organizations have a few people who consistently go above and beyond. Those people deserve recognition. But as leaders, we need to be mindful not to create a culture where success depends too heavily on a few individuals.

When one person becomes the unofficial owner of a process, a relationship, or a decision, the organization may benefit in the short term. Over time, that dependency can limit everyone involved.

It’s worth paying just as much attention to the people who make the whole team better. Who’s sharing what they’ve learned? Who’s mentoring new employees? Who’s building the processes that make everyone’s work easier?

Build a team that can grow together

Having talented people on your team is critical, but building a strong team takes more than that. They need the right tools, access to information, and the trust to make decisions together without waiting for a team lead to step in.

That changes the question we ask ourselves as leaders. Less “How do I make sure everything gets done?” and more “Am I building a team that can succeed without depending on me, or any one person?”

For me, that’s what leadership looks like as an organization grows. Less about being the person everyone needs. More about giving people the confidence, context, and support to achieve more together than any one of them could alone.
