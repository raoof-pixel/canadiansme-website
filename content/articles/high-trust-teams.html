In an exclusive interview with CanadianSME Small Business Magazine, Nadine Zeinoun Khoury, Executive and Leadership Coach, Team Coach, and Founder of Coach Nadine Zeinoun Inc., shares how emotional intelligence, trust, and authentic leadership can transform workplaces and strengthen teams.

Interview by SK Uddin

After a BS in Chemistry and an MS in Pharmacology and Therapeutics from the American University of Beirut, Nadine gained over 15 years of managerial experience in the corporate pharmaceutical sector. Growing up during the civil war in Lebanon profoundly shaped her perspective on leadership, resilience, and human connection.

You recently won Speaker Slam. What did you share on that stage, and why did you dedicate your work to emotional intelligence and leadership?

Winning Speaker Slam was incredibly meaningful because the story I shared, “The Road Less Travelled,” came from one of the most difficult moments of my career.

After more than a decade of promotions, awards, and giving so much of myself to an organization, I opened an email announcing a new structure, and my name wasn’t there. No conversation. No warning. No explanation.

I felt rejected, humiliated, and angry. The next day, I was supposed to lead an important product launch. Then I realized: I couldn’t control what had been done to me, but I could control who I became because of it. So I showed up.

To me, that is emotional intelligence, and that is leadership: choosing your values over your reaction, your character over your circumstances, and who you want to be over what was done to you.

How did your science and pharmaceutical background shape the way you understand disengaged teams?

My scientific background taught me to look beneath the surface, to stay curious and understand what is really driving an outcome. My 15 years in pharmaceutical leadership taught me to do the same with people.

I learned, the hard way, that disengagement is rarely just a performance problem. There is usually something happening beneath the surface: lack of trust, fear, uncertainty, feeling unheard, or a leader so focused on results that they’ve lost connection with their people.

I don’t approach leaders with judgment; I approach them with curiosity. I understand the pressure because I’ve lived it.

Having coached leaders on five continents, what patterns do you see in how they struggle with engagement, trust, and communication?

Cultures may differ, but the human needs beneath leadership are remarkably similar. People want to feel valued. They want to be heard. They want clarity, trust, and a sense that their work matters.

Under pressure, leaders often unintentionally create the opposite. Communication becomes more transactional. Listening decreases. Trust is rarely lost in one dramatic moment; it erodes through small, repeated experiences.

The lesson for me is simple: leadership is deeply human.

When a leader comes to you with a “disengaged team” problem, how do you help?

I rarely start with the team. I start with curiosity: what is happening beneath the surface, and how might the leader be contributing without realizing it?

Tools like EQ-i 2.0 and 360 help leaders understand their emotional intelligence, strengths, and blind spots. Conversational Intelligence helps us examine whether their conversations build trust or create defensiveness. Through coaching and programs like Authentic Influence, I help leaders turn those insights into everyday behaviours.

My goal isn’t to “fix” disengaged employees. It’s to help leaders create the conditions where people want to engage.

What practical first steps would you recommend for small and mid-sized businesses?

I recommend three simple practices: listen, create clarity, and build connection. Most importantly, start with self-awareness. Before asking, “Why is my team disengaged?” ask, “What might I be doing, or not doing, that is contributing to this?”
